# Design system

The rebuilt screens are built from what is here. The spec is
`docs/specs/frontend/first-time-play.md` (sections 7 and 8 override the text above them); the
look is `docs/design/` (mocks, `mock-to-skin.md`, art). Keep this file true when you change
what it describes.

## Where information a player asks for appears

**One rule.** Information a player asks for (their night, a role's details, and whatever a screen adds later) opens as a sheet from the bottom edge, in the one place, by the one primitive: `showSkinSheet` with a `SheetScaffold` (`primitives/sheet_scaffold.dart`) and its "Close" button (`RoleSheetCopy.close`). It is the same place whatever was tapped, never a popover or a card floating at the touch point, and it closes the same way every time: its button, a tap on the scrim, or Back (the sheet is a route, so `BackPolicy` closes it before the screen's own Back; it also closes when the screen under it changes). It is never dragged (a back swipe starts as a touch on the page). A sheet shows only what this player may know. Users today: a deck role (`RoleSheetBody`, a hold or a tap on a role on 02, 03 and 08). "My night" on 08 is no longer a sheet (the owner's square, 23 November 2026): it is held, and shows on the board from its top only while held, the same place wherever the note was held (`talk_vote_screens_test.dart` holds it at three points and asks for the same rectangle each time). Reduce motion: the sheet's own slide is the framework's; nothing of ours is added. Not yet moved to it: the held face of "You" on the night board (a hold, the face above the thumb: night, a held secret, the owner has not asked), and the night on 11 Results (a card in the page; the owner asked on 23 November 2026 for it to open as this sheet, open: that screen was another session's).

## The dress: plain, or paper (one switch per screen)

**Every component has a look in both dresses, listed in "Every component, in both dresses" below, and a paper one comes from ONE table (`skin/dress_style.dart`), never from its own code; paper draws no stroke.** The deck set-up page (03) is paper too (`DeckView.defaultDress`), so the host does not step from paper into plain.

A screen is **plain** (the look the game has today, exactly) unless it asks to be **dressed up**:
`ScreenFrame(dress: Dress.paper)` (`skin/dress.dart`; `dress: null` is as the screen above it, so
a preview or a test can wrap the app in a `DressScope` and see every screen dressed). The frame
declares a `DressScope`; below it `context.skin` is the dressed skin (`Skin.dressedUp`: its
colours, drops, one 5 px corner, angles, `skin.mood` and `skin.parts`; `skin/skins/shadow_paper_dress.dart`)
and every shared part below reads `context.dress` for what it draws or leaves out. **Three screens
are dressed up** (the owner, 23 November 2026: the table before the game, your card, the night
board: each declares `DressScope(dress: widget.dress ?? Dress.paper)` above its frame, `dress:` is
for a preview or a test); every other screen is plain, and the owner chooses the rest one by one. A sheet or dialog
opened from a screen carries its dress (`showSkinSheet`). Every game-wide scope (ground, dress, card back, Werewolf portrait) reaches a sheet or dialog the same way: `GameScopes` (`skin/game_scopes.dart`) captures them at the opening context and wraps the route's content, `showSkinSheet`/`showSkinDialog` are the only openers (`game_scopes_test.dart` scans `lib` for `showModalBottomSheet`, `showDialog` and the like, and for `art.role(` in a screen); a new scope is added in that one file. A screen's own code, above its frame,
reads `context.dress` too (the table, your card and the night board take `dress:` for a preview;
dressed up they leave out the picture or the village strip the mood stands in for).

The long version, with every token and value, is `docs/design/feel.md` section 1a. Every part
degrades to the plain one (a `PaperPin` takes no room, `PaperBanner` is its words, `deckledPaper` is
`cutPaper`), and every size, angle and colour is the skin's, never a number in a screen:

- **Paper shadow and the one light** (`skin/elevation.dart`, `primitives/paper_light.dart`).
  `HardShadow` is a hard offset copy (plain); `HardShadow.drop` is the paper shadow (a contact
  shadow plus a cast of five stacked flat copies: no blur, no saveLayer); `HardShadow.edge` is the
  paper's thickness and never leans. `Mood.leanOf` says which way a screen's shadows lean (night: the
  moon high on the right, so left; day: a low sun on the left; a lantern: straight down); `ScreenFrame`
  declares it as `PaperLight`, and `cutPaper`, `deckledPaper` and the cards read it (`litBy`).
  `skin_contract_test.dart` holds "no blur".
- **One file each** (`primitives/`, tests in `paper_parts_test.dart`): `deckle.dart`
  (`DeckleBorder`, a rectangle whose sides step in and out by a hair, seeded from the piece's id:
  paint only, the layout box and the hit target are the plain rectangle's), `deckled_paper.dart`
  (`deckledPaper`: fill, grain, torn edge, shadow; `deckledRing`: a pick's ring along the torn edge,
  never a rounded ring over it), `paper_pin.dart` (`PaperPin`), `tilted_paper.dart` (`TiltedPaper`:
  only the PAPER is turned by the skin's seeded angle, `Tilt.seededCard` at most 2.5 degrees,
  `Tilt.seededRow` 1.2; the words stay level), `paper_banner.dart` (`PaperBanner`: torn bone paper
  with two pins; `bleed` lets the paper reach past the words' box), `paper_mark.dart` (`PaperMark`,
  the one picked/done mark; `PaperMarkCorner` hangs it on a pick), `paper_piece.dart` (`PaperPiece`: the body of every paper component, from the one table `skin/dress_style.dart`; the primary button's amber on its backing is one), `fanned.dart` (`Fanned`: one card of a fan, turned and enlarged, paint only: a tap lands on the card's own
  untransformed slot. So that what is seen is what is tapped, its paper never reaches past its slot by more
  than half the row's `gap` toward a neighbour, nor the row above (`above`) by more than half of it, lift included: the skin's size and turn are taken down just until that holds,
  so no tap can land on the wrong card),
  `text_backing.dart` (`TextBacking`: a solid backing behind words that can sit over the village or a
  light), `lantern_pool.dart` (`LanternPool`), `paper_grain.dart` (`PaperGrain`, a seeded tile made
  once), and a picked row's mark and the sheet's torn top inside their own parts. The secondary button is a bone
  scrap (no pin, no outline).
- **`Mood`** (`primitives/mood.dart`; the palette is `skin.mood`, `MoodPalette`) is painted by a dressed
  `ScreenFrame` under everything: the ground, the game's own village (the layered art, the night
  village or the dawn gallows, at real strength in the top third and falling into shadow below), a
  moon's or lantern's pool, a vignette. **What it costs**: it is composed ONCE per (screen size,
  phase, light) into one picture, with its blends (`BlendMode.screen`, the moon's cast) done in that
  one pass, and kept (three are, the one it replaces disposed; a picture made without a layer that failed to load is shown
  but never kept; asking again while its layers decode starts nothing; a screen that closes mid-load gives its
  copies back; `mood_test.dart` holds each, and the cache stays at `Mood.kept` while a window is dragged
  through widths); a frame draws one image, with no layer saved and no full-screen
  blend. An animation above it, a press, the keyboard rising (the picture is the screen's size, not
  the body's) draw it no more (`mood_test.dart` counts `Mood.composed` and `Mood.painted`). Memory is
  one screen-sized image per kept picture, at the device's ratio up to 2. For a frame or two before the
  village's layers are decoded (art is precached at start) the ground and lights are painted live.
  A screen with a picture of its own (`topArt`, `topScene`) has no village in its mood. Bare words never
  sit on the village: they sit on paper, on a `TextBacking`, or on the plain ground; `mood_test.dart`
  measures the ground and its lights (7:1) and `dressed_contrast_test.dart` the real screens at 360 x 780
  and 390 x 844 with the village on, against the worst pixel round each word.
- **To see it**: `just paper-dress` renders every part in both dresses (`test_screens/paper_dress_test.dart`)
  and the table, your card and the night board in paper dress. `just paper-parts` is the parts page: every inventory component in paper dress in every state
  (rest, pressed, picked, locked, focused) on the night and lantern grounds (`test_screens/paper_parts_test.dart`,
  from `test/helpers/parts_catalogue.dart`); `just paper-components` is the table, your card, the night board and
  the card-details sheet as the live app has them, for before and after.

## Every component, in both dresses (the inventory)

`skin/dress_inventory.dart` is this list; `test/design_system/primitives/paper_components_test.dart`
holds the code to it: every file in `primitives/` is a row here or says why it is not a component,
every row has a sample on the parts page (`just paper-parts`), and no row paints a stroke on paper.
Paper looks come from ONE table, `skin/dress_style.dart` (`PaperPart`, read by `PaperPiece`, the one
body of every paper component); a component never draws its own fill, edge, shadow or border on a
paper screen.

**The one language for paper** (decided once, 23 November 2026, after "the paper look is so
inconsistent"): paper pieces are flat matte paper with a deckled edge and the two-layer paper shadow
of the screen's one light; NO strokes, outlines, inset lines or borders anywhere on paper (a piece's
edge is its shape and its shadow); no pin dots on buttons (pins only where something is pinned to a
board: notices, slips); the primary action is one piece of amber paper on a darker backing with
nothing drawn in it but its label; secondary actions are bone paper scraps (a lower lift, same
material); quiet actions are plain serif words with no underline; chips and tags are cut-paper tags;
picked and done are the `PaperMark` only (no ring); keyboard focus is a lifted shadow (no outline).
One locked form: a primary that cannot act yet ("Look first" before the card is looked at,
"Choose a card", "Need 3 players", "Add 1 card") is the QUIETEST button form there is, never the
brightest (art director, 23 November 2026: a pale bar read as a bone secondary button): dark indigo
paper (`PaperPiece.lockedPaper`: the ground's well, `PaperParts.lockedWell` of the way from the ground
to its panel, so it stands off the ground by 1.25:1 and lies lower than a chip), the same on every
part and every ground, barely lifted (`PaperPiece.flattened`: `lockedLift` of a scrap's lift,
`lockedShadow` of its ink, no backing), its label naming what unlocks it (never the live label) in the
ground's own text at 4.5:1 or more; `paper_rules_test.dart` ("locked") holds it lower than every live
button form in luminance and in shadow, and `paper_consistency_test.dart` scans `lib/` so a new locked
button is listed with Sheets and dialogs are opened from a context UNDER the screen's `ScreenFrame` (`FrameMark`; `showSkinSheet` asserts it): that is where the dress, ground and light are declared, so a card's sheet on the deck page and the table's "Leave" sheet come up in paper, lit as their screen is (`paper_rules_test.dart`, "sheets and dialogs": every opener in `lib/` is opened on its real screen, and a new one fails until it is listed). A card in the deck on 03 is told from one out of it in the paper language: IN is full colour on lifted paper with the `PaperMark` and a bone name tag; OUT is lowered paper, no shadow, its colour mostly gone (`PaperParts.outSaturation`), no mark, the name on the quiet navy tag. A role's name on a face-up card is always that navy `PaperPart.nameTag` (never a team's colour: red is the Werewolf team's `TagChip` alone, held by hue in `paper_consistency_test.dart`). A value box, an empty seat and a stepper's spent end are a `PaperPart.well`, paper sunk into the panel (the ground's own colour). Speed: every animated thing is on its own layer (`CueWave`, `HoldCue`, `FaceDownCard`: a layer for the moving picture inside a layer for the move), `DeckleBorder` keeps the paths it has cut, so the paper that holds still is painted once (`paint_cache_test.dart`; open: the pieces on the night board's own layer, its banner, own card and buttons, still repaint while the wave runs). its words. A team's tag is paper
of the team's own colour on every surface (the three team colours in `PaperParts`,
`TagChip.team`). The edge of a card is cut layers and shadow: a card back is a darker indigo layer under a navy
one (`PaperParts.cardRim`; never red: red is the Werewolf team's, painted only by its tag, rows and
markers and by artwork, `paper_consistency_test.dart` walks every paint call; `CardBackPaper`, the art's own
printed rim left outside by `CardBack.frameInset`), a token a
paper disc (`PaperParts.tokenInset`). Three tests hold it over every paper screen and every component
state (`test/design_system/primitives/paper_rules_test.dart`): SHADOW (every shadow falls down and away
from the screen's one light, `PaperLight`, which a sheet or dialog carries over in `GameScopes`; the
paper shadow is moved copies, never a spread, so there is no halo), SEPARATION (a piece differs from
what it lies on by 1.25:1 and casts a shadow; its words 4.5:1) and MATERIAL (pressed, picked, locked
and focused are the same piece as rest; only the colour moves). Only drawings that are lines by nature (the
QR code's modules and the scanner's camera frame) sit in a `Drawn` (`primitives/drawn.dart`), the
one allow-list the stroke test knows; the images (roles, card backs) are pictures and need none.
The paper mark's tick, the tally strokes, the seal and both lanterns are filled shapes of paper with
a shadow, never strokes. The plain dress
is every component's own code, kept exactly as it was (the owner has not asked for those screens).

### The end board's parts (piece 1 of the end board; no screen uses them yet)

## Scene changes (the dusk and the dawn)

`SceneSwitcher<T>` (`primitives/scene_switcher.dart`) is the table shell's one way to change screen. It holds the arriving screen and, for a change of `stage`, the leaving one, and runs a crossfade (`Motion.settle`: the new screen fades in over the old, which stays whole) unless `sceneBetween` names a `SceneKind`, then a scene (`Motion.scene`, 500 ms). The shell names two: **dusk** (02 or 03 to 04 or the board) and **dawn** (04 or the board to 08). Vote to count and every other change stay crossfades. Reduce motion (`Motion.within`, which the switcher reads): an instant cut, same information. The leaving screen takes no touch, no semantics and no Back (`BackMute`: Back goes to the arriving screen). A full-screen tap that only finishes a motion (10, 11) is no button to a screen reader (`excludeFromSemantics`); every button primitive (`PrimaryButton`, `SecondaryButton`, `TextAction`) carries its own tap in its semantics, so a screen reader can press it.

A scene is made of nothing but the two screens and a paper edge, no picture of its own and no dissolve: one screen is a sheet of paper that moves on a hard edge (a lip in the night's `paperEdge`, `SkinShape.paperLip`, and a hard `shadow`, `SkinShape.paperShadow` at its widest), over the first 85% of the scene (`Motion.curve`); the other stays in place.
- **Dusk, the night falls**: the card's screen is lowered over the table's from above, its foot leading on the lip and a shadow that shortens as it lands, while the table under it dims to half (`SceneSwitcher.dusk`); the card's header (04's layered village, `ScreenFrame.topScene`) comes down further than the sheet (`ScreenFrame.arrivalTravel`, 30% of its height), and in it, each in its own window of the scene's 500 ms (`SceneLayer.span`, over `SceneArrival.scene`, the scene's own linear clock): the clouds drift 16 canvas px into place from 150 to 425 ms, and last, from 250 to 500 ms, the moon is lowered into its sky from above the top edge (220 canvas px, 110 on a phone), so it is still coming when the sheet is within 2 px of rest (`scene_shell_test.dart` holds it).
- **Dawn, the day breaks**: the night sheet sinks off the screen on its top edge, its shadow thinning as it goes; the talk, whole and live underneath, has its own header come into its window (`SquareSky`, the layered dawn under a veil of the night): the hills, the middle roofs and the gallows on them rise from 300 canvas px below as one bank (so no edge of one shows free of what hides it), the near roofs 12 px further, the birds drift in (there is no sun: the square is lit by its lantern); the sky stays and the clock under it does not move.
The header pictures know the scene by `SceneArrival` (`primitives/scene_arrival.dart`): an inherited 0-to-1, eased (`progress`: the sheet's landing), and the scene's own clock (`scene`, linear over `Motion.scene`), only for the arriving screen while a scene runs; `ArrivalShift` moves a picture by its own height times what is left of it, and `LayeredArt` moves each layer from its `from` to rest (see below). A screen with no scene running is told nothing and sits where it rests. No layer is ever seen through another (a pixel test reads every frame of both scenes with flat screens and fails on a blend or on a pixel no widget painted). The primary button is on show by 60% of the scene (tested on both).

Rules the switcher keeps, each tested in `scene_switcher_test.dart` and `scene_shell_test.dart`: the arriving screen is in the tree and takes touches from the first frame, **where it will rest** (its slide uses `transformHitTests: false`; the leaving screen and the edge are `IgnorePointer` and out of semantics); nothing waits, blocks or delays a phase change (a scene is a slide of layers already painted, `RepaintBoundary` under each); a screen keeps its state from arriving to leaving (the same shape of widgets per layer, a `GlobalKey` each, so it can move into the sheet); a change that comes while a scene runs (05's wait giving way to the card) swaps the arriving screen in place and does not restart the scene; a scene that starts while a crossfade runs takes both of its screens into the sheet, which finish fading inside it (each change has its own controller). While `/me/` loads the shell shows the destination's own frame (`_CardWaits`: 04's village and headline, for the night's first screen after the deal; `_TalkWaits`: 08's sky, its dotted path and the headline, and no sun: where the talk is is not known until `/me/` says, and a sun drawn at the start would be seen leaving as the real one came), so a scene never lands on an empty ground and the screen then crossfades in on the same header. **Layered headers** (`LayeredArt`, `primitives/layered_art.dart`; the description is `LayeredScene` in `skin/art_set.dart`, the data in `shadow_paper.dart`; the art and how far each piece may move: `docs/design/art/layers/PROVENANCE.md`). A layer is a cut of paper with alpha, laid at its anchor on the canvas the layers were cut from, scaled and cropped exactly as the flat picture was (`SceneGeometry.cover`: cover, around the scene's focus), so at rest the layers are that flat picture (`layered_scene_test.dart` holds them to it). Brought in by a scene each layer travels from its `from` (canvas px) to rest in `Motion.curve` like everything else, over the sheet's landing, or, with a `span` (a share of the scene), in a window of its own, so one can arrive after another. Nothing moves by itself: there is no ticker and nothing idles, so a screen with it settles and night stays still. A shadow cast on another layer (`clipTo`: the clouds' on the moon) is drawn over it only where it has paint (`BlendMode.srcATop` in a layer the size of the moon, `_SrcAtopStack`), moving with its cloud, not with the moon. `LayeredArt` is its own repaint boundary, so the hold ring breathing on 04 draws the header once, not every frame. 04: the plate, the clouds' shadows on the sky, the moon, the shadows on the moon, the clouds; the clouds (and their shadows, on the sky and on the moon) start 16 px left (dark) or right (cream), the moon 220 px up. 08: the sky, the sun and its dotted path (`SceneSlot('sun')`: drawn by `TimerBand`, under the hills, so the path's ends and the sun at the start and end of the day are behind them), the far hills, the birds, the middle roofs, the gallows, the near roofs. The sun is the paper sprite on the path (`SceneArc`, in canvas pixels, the control raised so the sun clears the far ridge): `rise` (0.33) of the way along at 0 of the talk (just clear of the ridge), `set` (0.79) at 1 (60% of the disc still in view over the roofs: the timer's picture is not gone before time is up; `layered_scene_test.dart` measures it against the art), stepped by the clock with no tween; only a scene brings it up from `dip` (458) canvas px below, behind the hills. Nothing is added to a layer by the screen: reduce motion shows the layers at rest, instantly (no scene runs).
